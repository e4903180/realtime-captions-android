# Noise robustness (sherpa-onnx engine)

Generated by `:desktop` on 2026-11-27 (Windows 11, 20 logical CPUs). Engine: x-asr-160ms-int8 + VAD 0.65 + campplus-zh-en @0.5, VAD end 0.4s, ASR end 0.8s. Regenerate with:

```
python testdata/noise/make_noise_set.py datasets
./gradlew :desktop:run --args="--models <model dir> --noise datasets/noise_bench"
```

Speech is Taiwan-accent only: 4 Common Voice zh-TW two-speaker sessions and 2 zh-TW TTS dialogs (5.18 min), each mixed with every noise at every SNR (measured over the speech-active parts). Three runs: no denoiser, GTCRN, DPDFNet-2 (sherpa-onnx streaming speech enhancement in front of VAD, recognition and speaker embeddings).

## Verdict

- **Usable down to (CER < 15%, no denoiser):** kitchen −5 dB, street 0 dB, restaurant 10 dB. Babble, TV and reverberant café are never below 15% (clean is already ~8% on this Taiwan-accent set, and babble at 20 dB is 18–19%).
- **Worst noise:** other people talking — babble and TV. The recognizer writes their words down too (TV: CER over 100%), and in 3 minutes of pure TV noise it produced 43 phantom lines.
- **Denoiser: off by default.** Neither GTCRN nor DPDFNet-2 lowers CER (often raises it, e.g. restaurant 0 dB 39% → 68% with GTCRN), because the recognizer was trained on noisy speech and the enhancement artefacts confuse it. They do help speaker tags in restaurant/café noise (restaurant 0 dB: 57% → 74–82%), so a "denoise for speaker tags only" path may be worth trying later. GTCRN costs 0.09 RTF on one core here, DPDFNet-2 0.24.
- **Latency is stable in noise:** first text ~0.93 s, line finished ~0.8 s at every SNR.
- **Next step:** a near-speaker filter (loudness relative to recent speech + voice profile) to stop captioning TV and background talkers.
