# Offline engine benchmark (sherpa-onnx)

Generated by `:desktop` on 2026-11-27 (Windows 11, 20 logical CPUs, simulated real-time clock). Regenerate with:

```
pwsh -File testdata/generate.ps1           # synthetic dialogs + noisy variants
./gradlew :desktop:run --args="--models <dir with sherpa-onnx models>"
```

Test set: 6 synthetic dialogs spoken by Windows TTS voices (zh-TW: Yating, Hanhan, Zhiwei; en: Zira, David, Mark), 2–3 speakers each, 0.6–1.1 s pauses between turns, each in clean, babble and café noise at 10 and 5 dB SNR. Audio is streamed in 100 ms chunks.

**Metrics.** *Error rate*: character error rate for Chinese (after Traditional conversion), word error rate for English, mixed for code-switching; punctuation ignored. *First partial*: time from when a speaker starts until the first caption text appears. *Final*: time from when they stop until the caption line is finished (and gets its final speaker tag). *Speaker*: share of caption characters with the right speaker tag (best one-to-one mapping of anonymous labels). *RTF*: compute time / audio time for the whole pipeline (ASR + VAD + speaker embeddings) on this PC.

## Model selection

**Chosen: X-ASR zh-en 160 ms (int8) + Silero VAD (threshold 0.65, line ends 0.4 s after speech) + 3D-Speaker CAM++ zh-en speaker model (cosine threshold 0.5).**
This is now the default in the app (`SherpaOnnxEngine`) and in `:desktop`.

| Speaker | **3D-Speaker CAM++ zh-en (advanced)** | 28 MB | 3D-Speaker zh-cn + English | – | embedding per caption line, online clustering |
| Speaker | 3D-Speaker ERes2Net base zh-cn | 40 MB | 3D-Speaker zh-cn | – | same |

All candidates are mainland- or mixed-trained; none was trained specifically on Taiwan-accented Mandarin. Taiwan-accent results are in [benchmark-real.md](benchmark-real.md).

### Why this combination

- **Accuracy:** both X-ASR variants reach 1.4–1.7% Chinese character errors on this set, clean and in babble/café noise down to 5 dB, and ~0–1% English word errors. The 2023 bilingual zipformer is 3–4× worse (5.6–7.0% CER, 7–8% WER) and has no punctuation.
- **Speed of first words:** 160 ms chunks show the first caption text after **0.83 s** (p50) vs 0.95 s for 480 ms. For a deaf reader following a conversation, earlier words matter more than the slightly later line end (0.62 s vs 0.59 s).
- **VAD threshold 0.65 vs 0.5:** same accuracy on clean speech, but in noise fewer wrong line breaks and turn merges: CER at 5 dB 1.4% vs 1.7%, speaker tags in noise **98.0% vs 94.0%**.
- **VAD-based line ends are essential for speaker tags:** without them the recognizer's own end-of-utterance rule waits too long, consecutive turns merge into one line, and speaker accuracy falls to 52–59% (final latency 1.6 s p50, 5 s p90).
- **Speaker model and threshold:** CAM++ and ERes2Net give the same accuracy here; CAM++ is smaller (28 vs 40 MB) and covers English. Threshold 0.5 is the sweet spot: 0.35–0.45 merges different people (73–93%), 0.65 starts splitting one person into two (97.7%).

**Runner-up:** X-ASR 480 ms — same accuracy, slightly lower compute and quicker line ends, but first words appear ~0.12 s later (p90 1.21 s vs 0.94 s). It is a one-line switch if a phone turns out to be too slow for 160 ms.

RTF in the table below was measured while other jobs shared the CPU (the first row in particular), so treat it as an upper bound; all X-ASR runs are at most 0.2–0.4 on this PC.
